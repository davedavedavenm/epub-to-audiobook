## Australian English with a real reader for comparison

Graeme Dunlop reads the Australian entry in LibriVox’s *Celebration of Dialects and Accents*. Fish received the audio and transcript from 16.48–33.10 seconds. We then generated two different sentences from the same story. The human reading of those sentences starts at 47.71 seconds. The comparison plays the original first and the synthesis second, with matched loudness. LibriVox identifies the reader, labels the entry Australian and dedicates its recordings to the public domain in the US. This private experiment creates no Studio voice. [Original recording, entry 15](https://librivox.org/celebration-of-dialects-and-accents-vol-1/) · [LibriVox rights notice](https://librivox.org/pages/public-domain/).

The original lasted 12.55 seconds and the new reading 10.50 seconds. Unprompted Whisper small transcribed the same 32 words in order on both. That makes a direct listening comparison possible. The model uses Fish Audio’s research licence. We ran the community Q8_0 conversion with its BF16 audio component on a Radeon 780M, using llama.cpp b12823, seed 42 and the port’s sampling defaults. The delivered Cillian book used the separate upstream recipe. [Fish model and licence](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/s2-pro/tree/1de9996b6be38b745688de084d87a5633f714e4e) · [Conversion](https://huggingface.co/prithivMLmods/s2-pro-GGUF/tree/d705b61b423e4b654c458990a158f4d30aa4448b) · [Runtime](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/b12823/docs/multimodal/tts.md).

The same voice read Shared Test v1 in 69.96 seconds. Whisper recovered all 46 words of the ordinary opening when transcribed separately. Two recognizer sizes disagreed about how Fish spoke £26.45. Keep the amount flagged. The Fish-prepared text SHA-256 is `74de152cc1ed27890fbbcabc88f26d5cf360953e64e70a5d56d897ff1ceb0ee9`, and the standard MP3 SHA-256 is `30065517619e70d961f2718c968263b8e20ae7f0e5fee0b21f7cf88c09999f28`.

We kept the same 16.62-second fable reference and rendered 290 consecutive words from a different book that Dunlop also narrated, Rolf Boldrewood’s *Robbery Under Arms*. His performance lasts 110.35 seconds. Fish took 665.7 seconds to synthesize a 94.85-second reading. The largest mismatch in the transcription was two tokens, with no repeated passage. Whisper transcribed all 40 words in the closing comparison from each voice independently. Several earlier mismatches, including “mauleys”, occurred on the human reading too. The reader’s pace and expression are audible in the closing comparison, taken directly from the full performances. This remains an excerpt test. [Original audiobook, chapter 1](https://librivox.org/robbery-under-arms-by-rolf-boldrewood/) · [Text used for chapter 1](https://www.telelib.com/authors/B/BoldrewoodRolf/prose/robberyunderarms/robbery01.html).

The workstation keeps the paired ending in `evaluations/new-engines/output/regional-2026-11-28/book-ending-human-then-fish.mp3`. It runs for 24.84 seconds including the gap. The same directory contains the full excerpt from each speaker as `fish-book-opening.mp3` and `human-book-opening.mp3`. The private audio is excluded from Git.

## Flynn, trained on Australian audiobook performances

The author reports about 22 hours of Australian LibriVox audiobook and dramatic readings across eight speakers. Flynn is a preset learned during training. The readers’ identities and recording inventory are unpublished, so we lack a same-reader human comparison for Flynn. The checkpoint is Apache-2.0. The local run used the Q6_K community conversion, llama.cpp b12823 on the Radeon 780M, the upstream tokenizer, temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95 and the MIT 24 kHz SNAC decoder. [Author’s model, training and samples](https://huggingface.co/ablmontazer/orpheus-australian-en-tts/tree/2ca4be935e33f15484f8d19c7fd4e48e1ad60f24) · [Exact conversion](https://huggingface.co/mradermacher/orpheus-australian-en-tts-GGUF/tree/8af51ed6995928617723caff3fbcc7e75c64fa08) · [Pinned speech decoder](https://huggingface.co/hubertsiuzdak/snac_24khz/tree/d73ad176a12188fcf4f360ba3bf2c2fbbe8f58ec).

Across five newly written paragraphs Flynn read 266 words in 75.19 seconds. Every paragraph reached its end. Whisper medium measured 3.76% word error across the joined take. Four paragraphs transcribed exactly when checked separately. On the 65.35-second standard audition the two transcriptions of the closing date differed from each other and from the manuscript. Names also need review. Typing in dates or names by hand for each book would defeat the purpose of this evaluation. Flynn stays outside Studio’s approved regional choices. The raw shared-text SHA-256 is `386e062ef7020b882240cf512ba7c886be75b3d20dede6b60360b5c1bf3d8dc7`.

For internal screening, CommonAccent predicted US on all five six-second windows of Flynn’s sustained reading. It also predicted Australian on only one of five windows of the genuine Dunlop book recording and four of five on the Fish reading. These results have no role in approving an accent. The available listening comparison uses the actual human performance. [Classifier version and known errors](ACCENT-GPU-2026-11-27.md#can-a-machine-verify-the-accent).

## South African English from a Cape Town speaker

Baby Mynah uses an adapter for VoxCPM-0.5B trained on about eight minutes of read English from speaker p314. The University of Edinburgh's VCTK v0.92 metadata identifies her as a woman from Cape Town. The adapter author published the training duration and settings but did not identify the individual training utterances. The adapter and corpus use CC BY 4.0. The base model and the author's loading code use Apache-2.0. VCTK attribution: Junichi Yamagishi, Christophe Veaux and Kirsten MacDonald, University of Edinburgh, 2019. [VCTK corpus and speaker metadata](https://doi.org/10.7488/ds/2645) · [Pinned regional adapter](https://huggingface.co/build-small-hackathon/baby-mynah-south-african/tree/94b4586aa3c2aefb3225ef96bfae4dac0b180a43) · [Pinned base model](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM-0.5B/tree/c4cc061e4d3cbde5b33ea7ee65608eb5ba6f2b10) · [Author’s loader](https://huggingface.co/spaces/build-small-hackathon/baby-mynah/tree/e7a2ff968cba83881b205ff5c3b746b89e0180b0).

One unedited request produced 64.80 seconds of shared-test narration in 464.09 seconds on two i5-12400 CPU threads. VoxCPM 2.0.3 used BF16, normalization on, guidance 2.0, ten inference steps and seed 3236558577. Whisper small recovered the ordinary passage, 164 of 170 normalized words in order and the final April date. The differences clustered around numbers, units and currency. The WAV SHA-256 is `dda7d90960019555b79c6dacfeea6e584121a8553fe460f0ccd17d40f3a9bc5f`. This trial covers a minute of narration in a specific Cape Town voice. No audiobook or listening approval is recorded.

We next joined two of p314’s recorded utterances and asked the same model to read their transcript. The human recording lasted 11.00 seconds. Synthesis hit the runtime’s 262-patch limit, returned 20.96 seconds of low-level sound and yielded zero of 35 normalized source words in independent transcription. These utterances may have appeared in training. The successful first minute and failed short passage are both retained. Withhold this voice from the book shortlist.

Enabling only the upstream automatic retry caught the original failure. With the same input and seed, the runtime detected a 6.238 audio-to-text token ratio and generated a second take that lasted 10.72 seconds. Both the human reading and that take aligned on all 35 words. Total inference time including the retry was 217.51 seconds. This is evidence that an automatic retry can rescue a failed passage without the reader editing a word. Coverage across a chapter and perceived Cape Town accent remain open. The private diagnostic pair is `cape-town-human-then-retry.mp3` in the same workstation output directory. It uses two microphone-one VCTK recordings joined with a 120 ms gap, followed by the synthetic retry with matched playback loudness. [VCTK licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

All cited audio came from local CPU or Radeon runs. Two separate private Kaggle GPU trials were still queued when the local trials finished. They have 15-minute (Baby Mynah) and 25-minute (Orpheus) execution limits. No paid compute was used.
